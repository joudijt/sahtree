The Story Behind Berkat Madinah Store: From Ampang Shop to Charitable Mission

July 20, 2026 · 6 min read · By Sihatree

Food donation packing — part of the community work behind Berkat Madinah Store's growth.

Berkat Madinah Store began in 2010 as a single small shop in Ampang and has since grown into one of Malaysia's established Arab food businesses — and alongside that growth, it has kept a consistent thread of community giving, including orphanage support and food donation campaigns. Here's the story behind the name that now supplies Sihatree's Arabic Gum Powder.

How did Berkat Madinah Store start?

The business opened its doors in Ampang, Selangor in 2010, at a scale far smaller than what it's grown into today. What started as one shop serving the local Arab and Middle Eastern community has since expanded into a business spanning three sectors: wholesale distribution, retail branches, and restaurants and cafés serving traditional Syrian and Yemeni cuisine.

The company is led by founder Dr. Mohammed Ibrahim Abu Zaid, with Dr. Ibrahim Abu Zaid serving as Chairman. In the company's own words: "Your partnership and support are the foundation of our success, and we pledge to continue providing services of the highest quality."

What does Berkat Madinah Store stand for?

The company describes its mission as providing "diverse Arab food of the highest quality — meeting the expectations of Southeast Asia's population and the Islamic world," built around two core values: quality and credibility. Its stated purpose goes a step further — bringing "the traditional flavors of Arabia to every city in the world, to all those who cherish tradition and originality."

How much has Berkat Madinah Store grown since 2010?

From that first Ampang shop, the business now reports serving over 500,000 customers daily, stocking more than 6,000 products across 12-plus categories sourced from over 15 countries, and employing more than 150 people across its operations.

Why does a food business also give back?

For a company whose mission is framed around serving a community rather than just selling to it, charitable work and everyday retail aren't treated as separate things. Orphanage support and food donation campaigns sit alongside the same operations that stock spices, dates, and Sihatree's Arabic Gum Powder — not as a one-off gesture, but as a stated part of how the business describes itself.

That framing matters for buyers who care where a brand's money goes, not just what's on the shelf. A purchase through Berkat Madinah Store's retail or wholesale channels supports the same business carrying out that community work, even though Sihatree itself doesn't separately administer the charitable programmes.
